# Verified Source Notes for Biology- and Accelerator-Inspired Transfer Design

## Developmental Plasticity

Reh et al. report that critical periods occur at different times across developing systems and that properly sequenced windows may be important for higher-order functions; appropriate experience must coincide with the plastic window for circuit wiring. The article also describes rapid state changes, slower maturational processes, and longer-term baseline plasticity regulators. This supports a **staged, layer-specific replacement schedule** rather than simultaneous uncontrolled replacement of multiple modules.

Source: Reh et al., “Critical Period Regulation Across Multiple Timescales,” *PNAS* (2020), https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1820836117.

## Compensatory Plasticity

Raman and O’Leary analyze maintenance of learned function despite ongoing synaptic remodelling. Their framework distinguishes function-degrading fluctuations from compensatory plasticity and concludes that corrective change should be bounded rather than arbitrarily large. This motivates an **explicit bounded-loss or bounded-drift rule** while opening a new module’s plasticity window, rather than optimizing only local imitation loss.

Source: Raman and O’Leary, “Optimal Plasticity for Memory Maintenance During Ongoing Synaptic Change,” *eLife* (2021), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8504970/.

## Accelerator Staging

Lindstrøm’s review describes staged plasma-wakefield acceleration as an interface-matching problem: beam quality can degrade between stages without matching, alignment, synchronization, diagnostics, and an explicit stage-by-stage quality budget. The review emphasizes that local stage performance is insufficient if cumulative quality is not preserved. This motivates **interface operator matching, diagnostics before each additional layer replacement, and a cumulative degradation budget** for neural architecture transfer.

Source: Lindstrøm, “Staging of Plasma-Wakefield Accelerators,” *Physical Review Accelerators and Beams* 24, 014801 (2021), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.24.014801.
